1. Executive summary

What we are deciding. Only the contributor-facing layer: where the AI-guided interview runs, where code runs, who pays for the model, and who holds the materials. The scientific method is the same under both options and is specified separately: the interview protocol, the correct and flawed packages, verifier validation, and independent review.

What the analysis shows.

  1. The interface matters less than the shared method. Both options run the same interview protocol, produce the same schema-valid package, and feed the same internal pipeline, in which a benchmark-team shepherd turns each candidate into a verified task. The shared core costs about 10–17.5 engineer-weeks whichever option we choose. Beyond it, engineering effort is comparable: about 12–19.5 engineer-weeks for A and 11.5–20.5 for B.
  2. Money is not the main differentiator. The AI interview costs about $13–17 per contribution at API list prices (range $5–46), or about $3,400 for 200 contributions. Human effort and validation dominate: about 8,000 shepherd-team hours, 3,000 outside-economist hours, and $100,000 of validation-trial compute at 200 contributions (central estimates [U]). Whether the architecture changes shepherd hours is untested and could outweigh every other cost difference.

  3. Five real differences separate the options:

    DifferenceOption A (hosted)Option B (local)

    Under both options Anthropic receives whatever the model reads.

  4. Three constraints bind both options.
    • Regional policy. Anthropic’s Supported Regions Policy (effective 2026-11-12) does not cover mainland China, Hong Kong, or Macau, and its Usage Policy prohibits facilitating access there “(including end users)”. Neither option can serve contributors located there with Claude. The constraint is equal; the fallbacks are not (§6).
    • AI influence on experts. In a 2026 study, analysts with causal-inference experience followed an AI agent’s secretly randomized lean on design choices 86% of the time and rarely credited it. The interview protocol therefore measures influence rather than trusting self-reports.
    • Exposure of hidden material. The proposed rule is that any session that sees flawed variants or answer keys runs under commercial no-training terms. Option A meets it by construction. Option B then needs commercial-terms access from the project (if Anthropic’s terms permit it, O-018) or from contributors’ institutions; consumer plans are not enough.
  5. Other shared risks: shepherd capacity; contamination of tasks derived from public papers (evaluated models may have seen the paper or its package in training); and a name collision with an existing econometrics package called “deepecon”.

Recommendation (argued in §9, after the comparison).

  • Build the shared core first.
  • Start the legal and terms inquiries now; they gate both options (O-007, O-018).
  • Pilot minimal end-to-end versions (“thin slices”) of both options on the same paper, with symmetric thresholds registered in advance, plus a methodology arm that tests the construction pipeline itself.
  • If the group must choose before the pilot, contributors’ fluency with developer tools decides. For the cohort the research lead’s brief targets (economists who have published in the Top Five and leading field journals, including senior economists who may not use terminal-based tools), the lower-downside choice is Option A until the pilot’s setup test shows that they or their research assistants are fluent. For a fluent cohort, Option B with commercial-terms access.

Decisions needed now (§10): pilot both or a cheaper variant; the exposure rule; release policy; who pays for model access; legal and terms review; shepherd staffing; contributor credit; project name.

2. The two options in brief

Option A — hosted platform. The contributor signs in with an e-mail link or ORCID, attests their location, uploads the paper and code, and conducts the interview in the browser. A progress display and a live dossier panel show who said what. Code runs by one of three routes:

  • run kits on the contributor’s or a research assistant’s machine, including Stata (A1);
  • on staff machines during shepherding;
  • in a hosted R/Python sandbox for public data (A2).

The project pays for one pinned model under commercial terms. Variants under study: materials held in the browser until submission, an EU-region model route, and in-browser execution (Option A §3.3).

Option B — local skill. The contributor installs Claude Code, downloads the DeepEcon workspace (which registers the plugin), places the replication package in it, and opens it. A status line and a locally rendered dossier page show progress and who said what (to be tested in the desktop app). The model reads the local paper and code, drafts flawed variants in a separate folder, and runs them in the contributor’s own Stata, R, or Python. Layered safeguards keep the original package untouched, with a checksum check as the backstop. Variants: Claude Code on the web for public-data R/Python packages, Remote Control or SSH sessions that keep execution on a research assistant’s machine, and deferring operationalization to staff (Option B §3.2).

4. Key trade-offs

  1. Consistency versus reach. Option A gives every contributor the same interviewer, which makes contributions comparable and the protocol testable. Option B reaches people in their own tools and pins and records the model. Project-provided access, however, standardizes only the data terms and, with managed settings, the model; it does not standardize the Claude Code client (its version and system prompt) or contributors’ own configuration. Provided access moves inference (about $3,400 at 200 contributions) to the project. With comparable engineering, B’s remaining cost advantages are small hosting costs, a slightly lower maintenance range (0.1–0.2 against 0.1–0.25 FTE), and lighter custody during drafting, partly offset by more support and onboarding, higher evaluation inference (≈ $3,100 against ≈ $1,500 a year), and, with project-provided access, about $4 more inference per contribution than A1 or staff execution (desktop users need Team seats instead, at about $25–50 each for one to two seat-months [U]).
  2. Who runs the code. Both options can use the contributor’s own Stata. Option B lets the model iterate on a run within the session, which is where building a flawed variant and measuring its consequence is easiest; A1 needs a round trip per run, and staff execution moves the work onto shepherds. If research assistants do most of the execution, B’s execution advantage matters most for them. Symmetrically, if research assistants install and operate Claude Code, B’s setup burden also falls mostly on them, although the economist still installs Claude Code for the interview unless a Remote Control setup proves workable [U]. Good Benchmarks argues that domain experts and task builders are different roles.
  3. Custody versus dispersion. Under both options Anthropic receives whatever the model reads, and the project receives the submitted package. Option A additionally holds drafts, abandoned contributions, and transcripts on project servers and processors, with the security and data-protection duties that brings. Option B leaves drafts, and copies of hidden material, on contributors’ machines. A1 run kits also leave flawed-variant patches and outputs on contributors’ or RAs’ machines.
  4. Building and operating versus supporting. Engineering effort is comparable. Option A operates and secures a web service and its project API account, and supports run kits on contributors’ varied machines. Option B supports installation on varied operating systems and software, keeps a plugin compatible with a client that ships several releases a week (on its stable channel), runs a small upload service (and, with project-provided access, a project API account and Team seats for desktop users), and spends more on re-evaluating the interviewer per certified client release.
  5. Who pays, and on what terms. Inference is cheap either way. The substantive question is whether contributor sessions run under commercial terms (no training on inputs) or consumer terms (training unless the user opts out). For a benchmark with hidden answers that is a question of scientific integrity, so the proposed exposure rule requires commercial terms wherever hidden material is handled. Option A satisfies it by construction; Option B depends on project-provided access (O-018) or institutional accounts.
  6. Studying the interview. The protocol’s influence and drop-off measures are research outputs in their own right. Option A can observe every consented session and randomize centrally; Option B sees only what contributors submit, unless consented session metrics are sent to the project (as in the pilot; not yet part of the B design).

7. When each option is preferable

Option A is preferable if:

  • most contributors are senior and not fluent with developer tools, and their research assistants will not operate such tools either;
  • comparability of interviews, and research on the interview itself, are research goals;
  • the project can staff the security and data-protection duties of a hosted service over several years;
  • contributors are comfortable entrusting drafts and unpublished code to the project from the first session;
  • shepherds will handle most code execution.

Option B is preferable if:

  • most contributors, or their research assistants, are technically fluent;
  • packages depend heavily on local environments such as Stata, and in-session iteration on flawed variants matters;
  • commercial-terms access is available, from the project (subject to O-018) or from contributors’ institutions;
  • contributors prefer that drafts never sit on project servers;
  • the project prefers ongoing plugin maintenance and release certification to operating a hosted service that holds unpublished materials.

Neither option removes:

  • the regional constraint;
  • the need for shepherds, outside economists, and independent reviewers;
  • the release-policy decision;
  • the contamination controls and the exposure rule.

9. Recommendation

  1. Build the architecture-independent core now: protocol assets, schema, validator, packager, internal review store, and shepherd console. Every scenario needs them, and scientific quality depends on them most.
  2. Start the legal and terms inquiries immediately. Ask how the regional policy applies to the intended contributors and what diligence is expected (O-007), and whether the project may give external contributors Claude Code access, through API keys as its “authorized users” or through Team seats (O-018). Under the proposed exposure rule, the second answer decides whether Option B can run as designed without institutional accounts.
  3. Pilot thin slices of both options on one paper, using the pilot plan’s symmetric thresholds, registered before the first session. Why both: the decisive questions are empirical. Will senior economists, with designed onboarding, complete setup? Do run kits and research-assistant hand-offs stall? Do the options differ in validated variants, output quality, or measured AI influence? With the core in place, the second slice costs roughly 8–14 engineer-weeks [U] (v0.2 said 4–9), which is still small next to building the wrong full system and operating it for years.
    • If engineering capacity is the binding constraint, a cheaper variant builds one slice plus setup and hand-off probes of the other. It cannot compare output quality or influence between the options (O-019). Which slice is built in full must not be chosen for ease of implementation (D-005; pilot plan §3).
    • Either way, add the pilot’s methodology arm: every candidate whose flawed variant has been run and shows a material consequence (or a random sample of at least six, stratified by arm) goes through the validation ladder up to independent review (rungs L0–L12 of the verifier validation protocol §3), because the construction pipeline’s yield and cost are at least as uncertain as the architecture.
  4. If a choice must be made before the pilot, contributors’ fluency with developer tools decides:
    • The research lead’s brief targets economists who have published in the Top Five and leading field journals, including senior economists who may not use terminal-based tools. Until the setup test shows that they or their research assistants are fluent, the lower-downside choice is Option A (run kits plus staff execution). Its main failure mode, stalled hand-offs, comes after the interview and has a staff fallback, as B’s hand-offs do; B’s, abandoned setup, comes before any content exists. A’s own risk before any content exists is contributors’ reluctance to upload unpublished code (mainly when authors contribute their own papers, Path B); it is a §7 condition and may be reduced by holding materials in the browser until submission (Option A §3.3).
    • For a fluent cohort, or one whose research assistants are fluent and will operate the tools, Option B with commercial-terms access (from the project if O-018 permits, or from institutions) offers better evidence anchoring and in-session execution at comparable engineering cost.
  5. The end state may be H1, H3, or H4. Choose on pilot evidence.

  • Consistency credits B’s documented pin, block, and record controls, and states what provided access does not standardize.
  • Accessibility adds B’s variants and both options’ setup times; code execution credits A1’s use of the contributor’s Stata.
  • New dimensions and risks: research on the interview (A’s advantage); quality control enforced server-side versus by instructions; A’s engineering, operating, and account-suspension risks; B’s hidden-tier copies and research-assistant hand-offs; unequal China fallbacks; the terms risk split between project-provided keys and H2. The auto-mode risk was removed (the workspace disables auto mode).
  • The exposure rule makes commercial-terms access a requirement for B.
  • Costs: shepherd-team effort doubled to include dropped candidates; outside-economist hours and validation-trial compute added; route-specific inference; maintenance and evaluation inference for both options.
  • Recommendation: the research-assistant argument is applied symmetrically; the end state is left open among H1, H3, and H4; the second pilot slice is costed at 8–14 engineer-weeks, with a cheaper variant as an open question.

我们要决定的是什么。仅限于面向贡献者的那一层：AI 引导的访谈在哪里运行，代码在哪里运行，谁为模型付费，以及材料由谁持有。科学方法在两种方案下相同，并另行规定：访谈规程、正确复现包与错误版本复现包、验证程序校验，以及独立评审。

分析表明：

  1. 界面不如共享方法重要。两种方案运行同一套访谈规程，产出同样符合数据模式（schema）的贡献包，并进入同一条内部流程；在这条流程中，评测基准团队的任务引导人（shepherd）将每个候选项转化为经过验证的任务。无论选择哪种方案，共享核心约需 10–17.5 人周。在共享核心之外，两种方案各自所需的工程投入相当：方案 A 约需 12–19.5 人周，方案 B 约需 11.5–20.5 人周。
  2. 资金不是主要区别。按 API 标价计算，AI 访谈每份贡献约需 13–17 美元（区间为 5–46 美元），200 份贡献约需 3,400 美元。人力投入与验证占主导：200 份贡献约需 8,000 小时任务引导团队工时、3,000 小时外部经济学者工时，以及 100,000 美元的验证试验算力（中心估计值 [U]）。架构是否会改变任务引导工时尚未经过检验，其影响可能超过其他所有成本差异。

  3. 两种方案之间有五项实质区别：

    区别方案 A（托管）方案 B（本地）
    接入一个浏览器；访谈开始前约需 15–35 分钟，无需安装Claude Code（终端；或桌面应用的 Code 标签页，需使用 Team 或 Enterprise 席位）以及一个可下载的工作区；访谈开始前约需 31–66 分钟
    代码执行贡献者自己的环境（包括 Stata），通过运行包（run kit）执行，每次运行需往返一次；工作人员的机器；可选的 R/Python 沙箱贡献者自己的环境（包括 Stata），由模型在会话中迭代运行
    一致性整套访谈者配置（提示词、工具、模型、推理强度）在服务器端固定模型被固定、禁止切换并予以记录；客户端版本和贡献者的用户级配置各不相同，并予以记录
    保管项目方及其数据处理方自首次会话起即持有上传内容、草稿和对话记录；运行包会在贡献者的机器上留下错误版本及其输出的副本草稿保留在本地；项目方在提交时收到同样的贡献包；隐藏材料（错误版本、标准答案）的本地副本会一直保留，除非被清除
    对访谈本身的研究所有经同意的会话均可观察，包括中途放弃的会话；在服务器端随机分配只能从已提交的贡献包获取指标，除非会话向项目方报送指标；分组分配对贡献者可见，除非由项目方的服务端点提供

    两种方案下，Anthropic 都会收到模型读取的任何内容。

  4. 三项约束同时适用于两种方案。
    • 地区政策。Anthropic 的《支持地区政策》（Supported Regions Policy，2026-11-12 生效）不涵盖中国大陆、香港和澳门，其《使用政策》（Usage Policy）禁止为这些地区提供访问便利（“包括终端用户”）。两种方案都无法用 Claude 为位于这些地区的贡献者提供服务。这一约束对两者相同；备选方案则并不对等（第 6 节）。
    • AI 对专家的影响。在 2026 年的一项研究中，具有因果推断经验的分析师在设计选择上有 86% 的情况遵循了 AI 智能体被秘密随机设定的倾向，且很少将其归功于 AI。因此，访谈规程对这种影响进行测量，而不是依赖自我报告。
    • 隐藏材料的接触。拟议规则是：任何接触到错误版本或标准答案的会话，都在禁止用于训练的商业条款下运行。方案 A 在设计上即满足这一规则。方案 B 因而需要商业条款下的访问权限，由项目方提供（前提是 Anthropic 的条款允许，O-018），或由贡献者所在机构提供；消费者订阅计划并不足够。
  5. 其他共同风险：任务引导人人手是否充足；基于公开论文的任务可能受到数据污染（被测模型可能在训练中见过这些论文或其复现包）；以及与一个名为“deepecon”的现有计量经济学软件包重名。

建议（在比较之后，于第 9 节论证）。

  • 先建设共享核心。
  • 现在就启动法律与条款方面的问询；两种方案都取决于其结果（O-007、O-018）。
  • 在同一篇论文上试点两种方案的端到端最小版本（即“精简原型”，thin slice），采用预先登记的对称阈值，并增设一个检验构建流程本身的方法学组。
  • 如果团队必须在试点之前做出选择，则由贡献者对开发者工具的熟练程度决定。对于研究负责人的需求说明所针对的群体（在经济学五大期刊和领域顶级期刊发表过论文的经济学者，其中包括可能不使用终端类工具的资深经济学者），在试点的安装测试表明他们或其研究助理能够熟练使用之前，下行风险较低的选择是方案 A。对于熟练的群体，则是具备商业条款访问权限的方案 B。

当前需要做出的决定（第 10 节）：试点两种方案，或采用更省成本的变体；接触规则；发布政策；由谁支付模型访问费用；法律与条款审查；任务引导人的人员配置；贡献者署名；项目名称。
