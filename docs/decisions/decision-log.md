# Decision Log

This register is the single place where the project's decision status is recorded. Every item has exactly one status:

| Status | Meaning | Who can set it |
|---|---|---|
| **Decided** | A requirement the research lead has stated or approved. | Research lead only |
| **Proposed** | A recommendation awaiting review. Not binding. | Anyone; usually the research-engineering collaborator |
| **Open** | An unresolved question. | Anyone |
| **Rejected** | An alternative considered and not pursued, with the reason. | Research lead (or by explicit agreement) |

**Rules.** Proposals are never silently converted into decisions; a status change requires an explicit note of who approved it and when. Larger decisions with live alternatives get an Architecture/Methodology Decision Record in [`adr/`](adr/) using [`adr/0000-template.md`](adr/0000-template.md). IDs are never reused.

| P-003 | Classify every critical decision and failure mode by **validity layer**: computational (implementation fidelity), measurement, statistical, identification, or interpretation — the lead's three layers plus two. | P-004 | Assign a **verifiability tier to every graded output** (not per layer): V1 deterministic; V2a exact relation; V2b stochastic with stated error rates; V3 structured judgment with an expert-consensus key; V4 open-ended (unscored). Scored releases use V1–V2b, and V3 only after a set-level agreement study (Krippendorff's α or Gwet's AC1 ≥ 0.8) with item admission at ≥ 4 of 5 raters or a latent-class posterior ≥ 0.95. | Implements Safeguard C and *Good Benchmarks* #1/#6; econometric review. | [Verifier validation](../methodology/verifier-validation-protocol.md) §9 |
| P-005 | **First-release scope:** score error correction and research execution; score structured error detection only alongside clean controls; keep identification reasoning and interpretation in an unscored experimental track pending a V3 agreement study. | Outcome-verifiable today; trade-off stated in O-003. | Construction protocol §6 |
| P-006 | Introduce a **shepherd** role: a benchmark-team member owns task and verifier engineering for each contribution, from candidate to admission. | *Good Benchmarks* §9: domain expertise and task-building are different skills; shepherding works. | Construction protocol §2 |
| P-007 | Verify procedures through outcomes: agents deliver code under a benchmark-wide output contract; verifiers **re-execute it** (every graded quantity, never self-reported values alone) on **answer-changing hidden variants built on data the agent never sees**, and check exact relations: treated-cell weighting (effect injection) and counterfactual-side invariances (control span; instrument-orthogonal confounding). | *Partially* reconciles "outcome-verified" (*Good Benchmarks*) with "correct procedure" (Safeguard B): effect injection alone is blind to how the counterfactual is built, and visible-data variants admit a differential attack (econometric and methodology reviews). | Verifier validation §3, §5 |
| P-008 | Adopt the **validation ladder** (L0–L13, with a re-run matrix after any change) and the **acceptance-region** method for numeric tasks, including the six-way equivalence check. | A flawed implementation alone does not validate a task (brief §4). | Verifier validation |
| P-009 | Adopt the **task-quality rubric** v0.2.0 (36 criteria in 10 groups, 20 gates; the rubric's Who / when column is the single map from criteria to construction stages 0–8), adapting Terminal-Bench-Science's Apache-2.0 rubrics where they fit. | Operational translation of *Good Benchmarks*. | [Rubric](../methodology/task-quality-rubric.md) |
| P-016 | Task environments use open-source software (R or Python) by default; any Stata-only task forms a separately reported licensed track. | *Good Benchmarks* #7 (robust, pinned environments); a mounted Stata licence would be readable by the evaluated agent, and the terms for automated use are unclear ([R8](../research/evidence/R8-replication-standards-and-econometric-pitfalls.md)). Stata appears in 71.6% of packages, so most tasks need checked ports. | Construction protocol Stage 0 |
| P-019 | Bilingual principles: one protocol with localized surfaces; codes in data; originals preserved; reviewed EN–ZH glossary; English agent-facing tasks in the first release. Machine translation counts as a model call: an AI service may translate project material (D-039), contributors' material only with their consent (D-042); interface text follows D-030 and P-047 (revised 2026-11-07). | Brief §9. | [Bilingual design](../architecture/bilingual-design.md) |
| P-020 | Failure-mode **status** (established, conditional, contested) is set by blind classification by economists outside the contribution, after the materiality check and the first verifier rungs (RV-2, L4, L7): one classifier for items matching a vetted-taxonomy entry with a canonical citation, two for novel items. Each condition that is a property of the data is verified by a recorded diagnostic on the evaluation data and on every hidden-variant family used for grading; a condition that is a design statement rather than a property of the data (e.g., shock-based identification for shift-share inference, R8 P12; how the family of tests is defined, R8 P15) is fixed in the instruction, with any supporting diagnostics recorded. Default "needs review"; the contributor's view is recorded as a claim. | Structured expert judgment for contested questions (Cooke, SHELF, Delphi); avoids a single expert's call becoming ground truth. | Interview protocol §13; rubric GT-2 |
| P-022 | For alternative valid implementations: separate the estimand, identifying assumptions, and estimator; a hidden **decision space** (reference / accepted / unjustifiable / contested) with `requires`/`excludes` constraints and at most two multi-option decisions per task; declarations **only for non-crux conventions** from a benchmark-wide vocabulary, with crux decisions graded through outcomes on separating hidden variants; numerical noise, conventions, and alternatives handled by separate mechanisms, with no borrowed default band; inference graded deterministically against conditional references; thresholds set jointly for familywise false rejection ≤ 1%; full-harness replay; verifiers **frozen** before evaluation. | Many-analysts dispersion; CausalVerify's post-hoc scorer revisions; agents vary less than humans ([R5](../research/evidence/R5-analysis-causal-econ-benchmarks.md)). | [Verifier protocol](../methodology/verifier-validation-protocol.md) §4 |
| P-027 | **Derived data inherit the original licence.** Effect-injected, perturbed, and semi-synthetic data built on real covariates are hidden-variant techniques for redistributable data only; for restricted data the only route is fully synthetic data (every variable synthesized, with disclosure review and provider permission where required), on which the reference check, the materiality check, and the consequence are re-run. Task counts are reported by failure-mode category × data route. | Derived microdata would breach data-use terms (econometric review); fully synthetic data lose real-data quirks. | Verifier protocol §6; [construction protocol](../methodology/benchmark-construction-protocol.md) Stage 0 |
| P-028 | **Independent reference check:** a clean-room re-implementation from the paper, estimand, and codebook by someone who has not read the original code, or a correct blind expert solve; the open-source port used in task environments is a separate artifact verified for numerical equivalence; disagreements are logged as candidate natural errors. | A port inherits the original's errors (econometric review). | Verifier protocol L3; [rubric](../methodology/task-quality-rubric.md) GT-1 |
| P-029 | **Benchmark-wide materiality rule** (thresholds to be set by the research lead): a consequence is material if the estimate moves by at least one reference SE; the SE ratio lies outside [0.75, 1.33] or coverage falls below 85% at nominal 95% in a design-preserving simulation; the sign changes with \|t\| ≥ 1 under both; or significance changes with both p-values outside [0.02, 0.10]. Interpretation-only consequences need an independent reviewer's concurrence and tier V3. | The previous open-ended rule admitted knife-edge cases and missed SE-only flaws (both reviews). | Rubric RV-2 |
| P-030 | **Scoring and reporting:** binary reward per execution or correction task, conjunctive across checks and hidden variants; at least five trials per evaluated configuration; per task type, the mean with a bootstrap 95% CI, never pooled; repair and preservation scored separately; clean-control share fixed per release and undisclosed; models that touched construction marked *calibration-exposed*; release contents frozen. | Without a scoring rule, "change nothing" scores the clean share (methodology review). | Verifier protocol §7–§8 |
| P-031 | **Blind specification and solvability:** two economists outside the contribution, who have seen only the agent tier, write acceptance criteria from the instruction alone and attempt the task (`expert_solve`) at Stage 5, before verifier engineering; fallback: one blind solve per task and two on a random quarter of each task type. | People who know the answer cannot certify specification (*Good Benchmarks* §7). | Rubric SP-1, PR-2, DC-2 |
| P-032 | **Contamination controls for memorized methods:** a recognition probe gate (rubric LK-4); flawed variants and clean twins share a restructured presentation; answer-changing hidden variants on unseen bases; new tasks preferably from unpublished or post-cutoff papers, with both dates recorded (revised 2026-11-07: the held-out tier was replaced by D-040). | Perturbed data defeat memorized numbers, not memory of the paper (methodology review). | Rubric LK-2–LK-4; construction protocol Stages 0 and 4 |
| P-033 | **Taxonomy status corrections** before seeding the vetted taxonomy: split the regression-discontinuity entry (P10a global high-order polynomial, established; P10b conventional CI at the MSE-optimal bandwidth, established; P10c bandwidth choice, a decision-space node); add clustering by a discrete running variable as established; mark shift-share exposure-robust inference (P12) and multiple testing (P15) conditional and robust SEs in randomized trials (P14) contested or conditional; re-classify statuses at each release. | Econometric review. | [R8](../research/evidence/R8-replication-standards-and-econometric-pitfalls.md) corrections; interview protocol §14 |
| P-034 | **Difficulty calibration without tuning against evaluated models:** at least 5 trials × at least 3 configurations × at least 2 model families on the frozen task; pass rates reported per release, not targeted (revised 2026-11-07, D-039); a hint ladder (H1 design fact, H2 pitfall, H3 method) and a knowledge probe to separate capability from recall, with `recognition_task` tags reported separately while O-004 is open; no edit justified only by an agent's success. | Filtering tasks on the evaluated models biases their scores (*Good Benchmarks* §3, §9; methodology review). | Rubric SP-3, DF-3; verifier protocol L10–L11 |
| P-035 | **Privacy architecture for the MVP** (revised 2026-11-07 for D-042): self-hosted server; the browser talks only to the project server; no analytics, error tracking, CDN, web fonts, or embedded third-party content; outbound network blocked by default. An AI feature can be added later only through the gateway and the research lead's explicit configuration; it never receives restricted data, and contributors' material only with their consent. Calls are logged as metadata only. | D-042. | [MVP](../architecture/mvp-contributor-interface.md) §7 |
| P-036 | **Access and confidentiality:** random, time-limited, revocable invitation credentials stored hashed and never logged; server-side authorization on every page, API call, file, and export; contributors see only their assignments; candidates are answer-key material, never public and never in agent-facing exports; logs without contributor text or file contents; deletion on withdrawal and a defined retention period. | Team handover plan §6; leakage safeguard. | MVP §5.4 |
| P-037 | **Framing for authors:** the overview and every card state that candidates are deliberate, hypothetical modifications prepared to build benchmark tasks, not claims that the published paper contains errors; the paper's own acknowledged limitations are recorded separately (`issue_class`). | Authors review modifications of their own work. | MVP §3, §10 |
| P-038 | **Contributor actions:** per candidate, select one or both variants, modify (original kept), reject (optional reason), mark uncertain, skip, comment; propose additions at any time; *error* and *missing* variants shown with equal weight and no preselection; no quotas; original words preserved; each confirmation versioned and revisable until the team freezes the paper; an optional one-line reason when selecting; acceptance and rejection rates reported per paper. | Team handover plan §2; guards against easy agreement. | MVP §3 |
| P-039 | **Data model** from the example package V5: paper configuration, rubric import, contributor review, and submission manifest, defined as JSON Schemas with synthetic examples and tests in `schemas/contributor-interface/`. | D-023; matches the package's three item groups, tiers, and SHA-256 manifests. | [Schemas](../../schemas/contributor-interface/README.md); [MVP](../architecture/mvp-contributor-interface.md) §7 |
| P-041 | **Import the team's rubric file as it is**, whatever its field: the paper configuration declares the categories (label, one-line meaning, source array, which item fields fill each display role, which roles contributors see). | P-042 | **Runnable-package guidance** (D-027), following the AEA/SSDE Data and Code Availability Standard and template README: README with data availability statement, software, run time, and licences; complete code with a master script; dependencies; the data the author may share; a log of the author's own complete run with its outputs; the results map; original file names. | P-043 | **Four storage tiers**, mirroring the package: agent-facing (candidate ZIP), expert-only (rubric, guides), researchers-only (pilot reports; never shown to contributors), contributor-submitted. | P-044 | **Layered item card** (answers the lead's Q1 question): headline and one-line summary; local change (original and modified excerpt, with original file names and lines); why it matters (affected results, consequence); what a correct answer must do (one click); scoring hidden by default. | P-045 | **Map and item panel on one screen** (answers Q3): the workflow (or, for theory papers, the argument) as stages with item markers and a results column, beside the selected item's local change; markers and the panel stay in sync; a matrix toggle (results × items); a list on phones; paired error/missing variants joined. | P-047 | **Interface languages:** all interface text in translation catalogues (gettext), one per language; drafted with an LLM (interface text contains no project material) and reviewed by the team; per-paper labels localized by BCP 47 tag; a language is added by one reviewed catalogue, without code changes. | D-030. | [MVP](../architecture/mvp-contributor-interface.md) §6.3 |
| P-049 | **Restricted data in contributed papers** (revised 2026-11-06, after O-034 was closed): first read the package's data availability statement and, from the contributor, the data editor's report if there is one, since some inputs may be public or shareable. The default route for a dataset the author may not share is a **run kit** that the contributor (usually the paper's author) or their RA runs where the data already are, if they still have access [U]: the reference code, all confirmed items applied together, and one version per confirmed item; one settings line for the data path; one script that builds the data once and reruns each version from its first changed step; an output check that flags small cells. They return logs, tables, figures, and a file list with SHA-256, never data. The team tests the kit first on a synthetic stand-in built from the codebook. Fallbacks: the data provider's own remote-execution service, if it has one [U]; an access application; a secure environment; and, labelled as such, a static check plus the synthetic run. The kit is answer-key material, kept confidential under O-026 (revised 2026-11-07: held-out clause removed). The demo paper needs no data. | One round with the author, usually no new data agreement [U], and nothing restricted leaves the authorized environment. | MVP §5.4 |
| P-050 | **Data-sharing check (O-035):** uploads that include data stay quarantined (encrypted, unopened) until a named data steward has checked each dataset's terms against its source; passing data are released to the team, failing data deleted and the contributor offered the author-runs route; each check logged. | Holding data the provider's terms forbid would breach them. | MVP §5.5 |
| P-060 | **How the paper pool works** (defaults Claude chose to build D-044; each can be changed). (a) The papers offered to a contributor are their assignments, which the team creates (for an author, their own paper), and the papers the team marks "open to all contributors"; nothing is open to all unless the team marks it. (b) Before choosing, a contributor sees only each paper's citation; the items, the map, and the package appear only after choosing. (c) Every choice and change is written to the event log, so the team knows who has seen which task's items. (d) Changing paper keeps the work and consent on the earlier paper, and the contributor can return to it. (e) Several contributors may choose the same paper. (f) A paper chosen from those open to all is recorded as "invited expert"; only the team records authorship, and the notice then says "this paper" rather than "your paper". (g) Each contributor has one active link: issuing a new link revokes the earlier ones and ends sessions opened with them. (h) A link created from an assignment opens that paper; a link created for a contributor with no current paper leads to the list of papers. | Every item of a scored task is answer-key material (Rule 8): opening a scored task to all contributors lets every invited contributor see its items, so the team decides that per paper, and the event log records who saw what. Keeping one link and one paper at a time is what the lead asked for, so contributors are not confused. | D-044; [implementation plan](../architecture/mvp-implementation-plan.md) §2.2–2.3 |
