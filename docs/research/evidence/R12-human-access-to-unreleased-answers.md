## 1. Short answer

*(assessment)*

1. **Terminal-Bench-Science has no closed pool at all.** Every proposal, task PR, solution and test is public before and after merge, and anyone, contributor or not, can browse every task. TBS has no scored-versus-unpublished distinction to protect: it publishes its answer keys and relies on a canary string, the agent's "do not cheat" instruction, and an LLM audit of trials. Terminal-Bench 2.0 and the current Terminal-Bench (3 and 4) work the same way. By TBS's standard, DeepEcon's "open to all contributors" setting is far more restrictive.
2. **Benchmarks that keep answers unreleased are the right comparison for DeepEcon's scored tasks**, whose answers stay unpublished (D-040, Rule 8). In every such case found, the unreleased material is held by the organizing institution. People other than the core team see a task only by assignment: its author, one or more assigned reviewers or validators, or a QA tester. **No benchmark was found in which every contributor may browse other contributors' unreleased tasks or answer keys.**
3. **Cross-review by other experts is common, but by assignment.** GPQA's question writers validated one another's questions (46 of 47 writers in the main set also served as expert validators of others' questions). TBS assigns a domain reviewer to each task. METR has a QA tester complete each task. HealthBench Professional had a second physician review each example [S].
4. **Confidentiality rests mostly on requests, not contracts.** Formal agreements were found only at FrontierMath (contractor NDAs [S]) and PaperBench (a "formal agreement" with each paper's author, terms not described [S]). Elsewhere there are requests: do not publish, do not redistribute, keep repositories private, avoid services that train on inputs (METR, GPQA's dataset card, GAIA, ScienceAgentBench).
5. **No benchmark documents logging which people saw which unreleased task.** TBS and Harbor Hub record who was *granted* access to trial jobs, not who read what. The site's event log, which records every paper choice (P-060 (c)), already goes further than any documented practice.
6. **For DeepEcon:** opening a demo or development task to all contributors matches TBS's practice, since such tasks may be published anyway. Opening a scored task to all goes beyond what benchmarks with unreleased answers do. Their tradition is to keep such tasks with the core team and show them to assigned people only. Proposal P-061 follows from this.
